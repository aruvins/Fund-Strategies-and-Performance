propositions about how leverage constraints affect asset pricing.

WHAT EACH PROPOSITION TESTS
============================

PROPOSITION 1: High Beta → Low Alpha
────────────────────────────────────
Test:       Do high-beta assets have lower risk-adjusted returns?
Method:     Form 5 beta-sorted quintile portfolios
Metric:     Alpha (CAPM and Fama-French), Sharpe ratio
Expected:   Alpha and Sharpe decline monotonically with beta
Result:     Graph showing alpha vs beta quintile, declining trend
Files:      proposition_1_results.png
            proposition_1_detailed_results.csv

Example Output:
  P1 (Low Beta):  Beta=0.65  Alpha=+0.40%/month  Sharpe=0.70
  P2:             Beta=0.85  Alpha=+0.25%/month  Sharpe=0.63
  P3:             Beta=1.00  Alpha= 0.00%/month  Sharpe=0.57
  P4:             Beta=1.15  Alpha=-0.15%/month  Sharpe=0.51
  P5 (High Beta): Beta=1.50  Alpha=-0.40%/month  Sharpe=0.33
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  DIFFERENCE (P1-P5): -0.80%/month = -9.6% annualized

PROPOSITION 2: BAB Factor Positive Returns
───────────────────────────────────────────
Test:       Does a market-neutral BAB factor generate abnormal returns?
Method:     Construct BAB = long low-beta (leveraged) - short high-beta (deleveraged)
Metric:     Alpha, Sharpe ratio, market beta
Expected:   Significant positive alpha, Sharpe >0.60, beta near zero
Result:     Time series of BAB returns with performance metrics
Files:      proposition_2_results.png

Example Output:
  Annualized Return:     6.5%
  Annualized Volatility: 10.8%
  Sharpe Ratio:          0.70
  CAPM Alpha:            +0.50% monthly (+6% annualized) ***
  FF3 Alpha:             +0.42% monthly (+5% annualized) **
  Market Beta:           0.05 (near zero, as intended)
  ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  t-statistic on alpha:  4.2 (highly significant)

PROPOSITION 5: Investor Heterogeneity
──────────────────────────────────────
Test:       Do constrained investors hold higher-beta portfolios?
Method:     Classify investors (growth vs value), compare portfolio betas
Metric:     Average beta, beta difference, t-test
Expected:   Constrained beta > Unconstrained beta, significant difference
Result:     Box plot and time series showing beta difference
Files:      proposition_5_results.png
            proposition_5_detailed_results.csv

Example Output:
  Constrained Investors (Growth): Beta = 1.18
  Unconstrained Investors (Value): Beta = 0.88
  Difference: 0.30 (30% higher volatility)
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Paired t-test:
    t-statistic: 12.3
    p-value: <0.0001 (highly significant)
    95% CI: [0.26, 0.34]

COMMON QUESTIONS
================

Q: What if my results don't match expected values?
A: Possible causes:
   1. Different data period (results vary by era)
   2. Different stock sample (small-cap vs large-cap effects differ)
   3. Different beta estimation method
   4. Data quality issues (check for missing data)
   5. Shorter sample period (need 10+ years minimum)
   
   First try: Check data quality and sample size

Q: Can I modify the methodology?
A: Yes! The code is fully customizable:
   • Change beta window (36-60 months)
   • Change portfolio formation (deciles vs quintiles)
   • Change weighting (value-weight vs equal-weight)
   • Add more factors to alpha calculation
   See README.md "Customization Guide"

Q: How long does analysis take?
A: 
   Synthetic data (500 stocks, 50 years): ~5 minutes
   Real data (3,000 stocks, 50 years): ~30 minutes
   Depends on computer speed and data size

Q: Can I test Propositions 3 & 4?
A: Yes, but modified versions (LIBOR deprecated):
   • Proposition 3: Use Fed funds rate, credit spreads
   • Proposition 4: Use VIX, financial stress indicators
   See README.md "Advanced Topics"

Q: Should I use daily or monthly data?
A: Monthly is recommended:
   • More stable beta estimates
   • Less noise in returns
   • Standard in academic literature
   • Shorter computation time
   Daily data works but requires more careful handling

Q: How many stocks do I need?
A: Minimum 200, recommended 500+:
   • Ensures robust portfolio formation
   • Reduces idiosyncratic risk
   • Allows meaningful alpha calculation
   • More realistic market representation

Q: How far back should data go?
A: Minimum 20 years, ideally 50+:
   • Need enough observations for beta stability
   • Can show variation across market conditions
   • Publishable research standard
   • Original paper uses 1926-2012 (86 years)

KEY TAKEAWAYS
=============

Main Finding: Leverage constraints create a profitable mispricing
  → High-beta assets are overbid (by constrained investors)
  → Low-beta assets are underbid (relative to unconstrained demand)
  → Creates alpha opportunity (BAB factor)
  → Effect visible in investor behavior (Prop 5)

Practical Implication: Low-beta stocks outperform in risk-adjusted terms
  → Can be exploited by unconstrained investors (hedge funds, PE)
  → Explains why smart money buys "boring" stocks
  → Consistent with Buffett's strategy (low-beta, leveraged)

Theoretical Implication: Leverage constraints matter for asset pricing
  → Standard CAPM incomplete without considering constraints
  → Security market line flatter than theory predicts
  → New factor: funding liquidity risk (BAB premium)
